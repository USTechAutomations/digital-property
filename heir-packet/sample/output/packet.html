Provider death or inactivity steps

Matched by website domain only. A missing match or UNKNOWN is not proof that a provider has no process. Check official help before acting. These summaries do not grant account access.

Google

Beforehand, configure Inactive Account Manager and choose the data and contacts. After a death, the family can use Google’s deceased-user request process for closure, funds or data. Google does not supply passwords; requests are reviewed.

https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/3036546

https://support.google.com/accounts/troubleshooter/6357590

Official help checked 2026-10-03.

eBay

Open eBay’s deceased-member help page and use its report form. Follow the prompts to identify the account and explain the request.

https://www.ebay.com/help/account/changing-account-settings/reporting-death-ebay-member?id=5574

Official help checked 2026-10-03.

Pinterest

Contact Pinterest through its official help process to request deletion of a deceased family member’s account, with verification such as an obituary or death certificate. Pinterest does not disclose personal or login information.

https://help.pinterest.com/en/article/deactivate-or-close-your-account

Official help checked 2026-10-03.

Apple

Beforehand, add a Legacy Contact in Apple Account settings and arrange to keep the access key separately. The contact requests access with that key and a death certificate. Keychain passwords, payment details and passkeys are excluded.

https://support.apple.com/en-us/102631

Official help checked 2026-10-03.

Password-manager reference

  • Bitwarden: In Emergency Access, invite a trusted person, have them accept, then confirm them. Set a wait and view or takeover access. Eligible paid plans can grant it; the contact requests access from their own vault.

    https://bitwarden.com/help/emergency-access/

  • 1Password: Prepare the separate Emergency Kit using 1Password’s instructions, and keep it securely accessible to your trusted person. It contains secrets: never attach it to this packet. This is not an automatic timed handover.

    https://support.1password.com/emergency-kit/

  • LastPass: In your vault’s Emergency Access section, choose a trusted LastPass user and a waiting period. Arrange acceptance ahead of time. The trusted person later requests access from their own vault. Check plan eligibility.

    https://www.lastpass.com/features/emergency-access

  • Proton Pass: Proton’s paid-plan Emergency Access is set in account Recovery settings. Add a trusted Proton contact and choose the wait. The contact later requests access from their Recovery settings. Check any extra Pass password arrangements separately.

    https://proton.me/support/emergency-access

  • Apple Passwords: Apple Legacy Contact covers eligible Apple data, but NOT Keychain passwords or passkeys. Set up Legacy Contact in Sign-In & Security for eligible data; arrange password access separately.

    https://support.apple.com/en-us/102631

  • Chrome / Google Password Manager: Configure Google Inactive Account Manager for the eligible data and contacts it lists. Do not assume it hands over your saved passwords. Keep separate password-access instructions in a secure place.

    https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/3036546

  • Microsoft Edge: The export guide is not an emergency-access feature. Read Microsoft’s deceased-account guidance and arrange separate password-access instructions while you can. This packet cannot unlock Edge.

    https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/account-billing/accessing-outlook-com-onedrive-and-other-microsoft-services-when-someone-has-died-ebbd2860-917e-4b39-9913-212362da6b2f

  • Firefox: The export guide documents an unprotected CSV, not a legacy handover. No automatic emergency-access process is confirmed here. Arrange access instructions separately; never leave the exported CSV for your family.

    https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/export-login-data-firefox

  • Dashlane: No automatic legacy-access process is confirmed here. Make separate access arrangements with your trusted person. Its CSV export is unprotected and must be deleted after use.

    https://support.dashlane.com/hc/en-us/articles/32905278138002-Export-your-Dashlane-data-to-a-CSV

Remove the unprotected export

Delete the original export after checking your packet. On Windows, select the export in File Explorer, press Shift+Delete and confirm. On Mac, move it to Trash and empty Trash. On Linux, delete it and empty Trash. Also remove any Dashlane export ZIP and extracted export folder, and any copies in synced folders or backups you control. Deleting is not a guarantee of secure erasure on SSDs or backups. Keep exports off shared computers, email and cloud-synced folders. The packet still reveals account names and usernames: store it privately.
